# Journal - July 25, 2026 - Day 1: Making Data Useful

## Today in one sentence
- Day 1 was about understanding what it means to make data useful, from preparing and improving its quality to analyzing it and using it to support better decisions.

## What I learned
- Before starting my Data Engineering journey, most of my professional experience was in operations administration and customer service. I was comfortable working with processes, spreadsheets, data, and different business systems, but Data Engineering was a completely new area for me.

- When I applied for the **Accenture x FTW Data Engineering Program**, I knew that I was entering something outside my comfort zone.

- I was excited, but also unsure of how much I would actually be able to learn.

- I decided to approach the program with one mindset:

  > **I don't need to know everything immediately. I just need to keep learning and understand why I'm doing each step.**

- At the very beginning of the program, we were asked:

  > **"What do you think a Data Engineer does?"**

  My answer at the time was:

  > **"Move data from one place to another."**

  Looking back, this answer was simple, but it reflected what I understood about Data Engineering at that point.

  I knew that data had to move from one system or location to another, but I didn't yet understand everything that happens around that process.

- One of the biggest things I learned today is that **making data useful involves much more than moving it**. We need to understand the problem, identify the relevant data, check its quality, prepare it properly, analyze it, and eventually use the results to help answer questions and support decisions. The session presented this as a process that starts with defining the problem and getting relevant data, followed by checking and treating data quality issues, preparing the data, analyzing it, and interpreting the results.

- I learned that **data preparation** is the broader process of making raw data ready for analysis or modeling. It can include cleaning, integrating, formatting, validating, and splitting datasets. **Data transformation** is more specific and focuses on changing the format, structure, or values of data.

- Data quality was another important part of today's lesson. The session introduced several data quality attributes: **consistency, accuracy, completeness, auditability, validity, uniqueness, and timeliness**.

- I found the examples helpful because they made the concepts easier to understand. For example, a duplicate Transaction ID is a **uniqueness** issue, a blank Customer Type is a **completeness** issue, and having "GCash", "Gcash", "GCASH", and "gcash" represents a **consistency** issue.

- I also learned that addressing data quality issues can be thought of as four steps: **Assess, Understand, Fix, and Prevent**.

- The afternoon session introduced **Data Analysis**. I learned that analysis is not simply looking at numbers or creating charts. It involves working with data to find useful information that can help answer questions and support decision-making. The session also introduced four main types of data analysis: **descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive**.

- What stood out to me was the idea that before analyzing data, I need to understand **what problem I am trying to solve**. The exercise asked questions such as the top-selling product, which retailer sold the most of it, where it sold the most, and which customers made the most transactions or spent the most. This showed me that analysis starts with questions, not with randomly looking at data.

## Terms I am still learning
- **Data Preparation** - The broader process of making raw data reliable, consistent, and usable for analysis or modeling.
- **Data Transformation** - Changing the format, structure, or values of data so it can be used for a specific purpose.
- **Data Quality** - The characteristics that determine whether data can be trusted and used properly.
- **Data Analysis** - Working with data to discover useful information, answer questions, and support decisions.
- **Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)** - Investigating data to discover patterns, identify anomalies, test hypotheses, and check assumptions.

## What confused me
- I initially thought Data Engineering was mainly about moving data from one place to another. Today made me realize that there are many important steps around the data before it becomes useful.

- I am still trying to understand where the responsibilities of a Data Engineer begin and end, especially the relationship between data preparation, data transformation, data analysis, and other parts of the data workflow.

- I also need more practice distinguishing the different data quality issues. Some of them sound similar at first, so I want to become more comfortable identifying whether a problem is related to accuracy, completeness, consistency, validity, uniqueness, or timeliness.
